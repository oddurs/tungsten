## What to add

Things people measure against: coffee, banana, paperclip, bathtub, Olympic pool,
football field, Boeing 747, blue whale, human lifetime, Mount Everest… Each with
the properties people actually ask about, plus a `default` property for
`N <item>s of <quantity>` queries.

## Sources

USDA FoodData Central for food; manufacturer specs; cite each.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Every entry has a `source`
- [x] `w --why coffee.caffeine` shows provenance
- [x] Each item tagged whether it is a good for-scale reference

## 2026-09-23

Done, with fewer items than the title's ~150 and no padding: 112 items. 43 foods are generated by scripts/data/food.py from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (the April 2018 CSV bundle): each is a published household portion, with energy, caffeine and sugar scaled from per-100 g values to 3 s.f. 69 things are hand-written in data/items.toml: sport and standards (IFAB, ITF, NFL, ISO 216, ISO/IEC 7810, ISO 668, IEC 60086), US Mint and ECB coins, buildings, vehicles, nature. Before writing them I checked the uncertain ones against their sources (NOAA, NASA, NPS, USBR, EPA, USGS, ESB, Golden Gate District, Central Park Conservancy, Walpole 2012, WHO, Wikipedia infoboxes) and dropped what did not check out: bathtub (no source found), dollar bill, Saturn V, Great Barrier Reef, Nile, elephant, giraffe, peregrine falcon, F1 car, Little Boy. Corrections from checking: cheetah speed is '60-70 mph' (midpoint used), the ISS's 356 ft is solar-array wingspan not length, and WHO's latest life expectancy is 71.4 years (2021), not 73.3. Every entry has a source; '--why coffee.caffeine' snapshot; for-scale references are tagged with 'scale' (34 of them) and grouped by 'domain'. Build check tightened along the way: entity names may not equal any property alias (this caught 'lifetime'). Names may now include numbers (boeing 747, iphone 15).
